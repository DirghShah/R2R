Most advice about organising saved TikToks assumes the folder is salvageable. If
you have four hundred saves, it mostly is not — and the fastest way forward is to
accept that and start from a different place.

## First, be honest about what a saved post is

A save is a bookmark with no metadata. No name, no address, no date, no reason.
You saved it because of something that happened in the video, and the save
records none of it. That is why the folder gets big: adding to it is free, and
getting anything back out is expensive.

Any system that does not fix that asymmetry will fail the same way.

## Step 1: Split by what you actually want back

Go through your saves once and sort into three buckets. Do not try to organise
within them yet.

- **Places** — restaurants, cafés, bars, shops. These need an address to be
  useful, which means they need to leave TikTok.
- **How-to** — recipes, workouts, techniques. These need to be findable by
  keyword, which means text.
- **Everything else** — things that made you laugh, outfits, things you meant to
  buy. Be ruthless here. Most of it can go.

Most people find the third bucket is over half the folder, and that deleting it
costs them nothing at all.

## Step 2: Use TikTok's own collections, but only as a staging area

TikTok lets you organise saves into Collections. Long-press a saved video → *Add
to collection*.

**This is worth doing** — but as a queue, not a filing system. Make one called
"To process". Collections still do not give you an address or a searchable name;
they just make the pile smaller and more legible.

The mistake is building an elaborate collection hierarchy and believing the
problem is solved. It is not — you have organised bookmarks, not information.

## Step 3: Get places out of TikTok entirely

This is the step that actually works, because a restaurant is only useful when it
is somewhere you will look when hungry: a map.

Three options, in ascending order of effort saved:

- **Manually**, into a Google Maps list. Free, reliable, and about ninety seconds
  per place. Fine below five a month.
- **Screenshot-based apps**, which OCR a frame. Fast, but a screenshot is one
  moment and has no audio.
- **Share the reel to an app that reads it**, which handles multi-place videos —
  the "9 cafés in Dallas" case that breaks everything else.

Whichever you pick, the test is what happens to a video listing several places.
That is where tools separate.

## Step 4: Fix the intake, or you will be here again in six months

The backlog came from saving being one tap and retrieving being twenty minutes.
If you clear the folder without changing that, it refills at exactly the rate it
did before.

**Make the useful action as cheap as the useless one.** If getting a place onto
your map is also one tap — share, done — then the saved folder stops being a
dumping ground, because there is a better option that costs the same.

That is the whole trick. Not discipline, not a better folder structure. Just
making the right thing as easy as the wrong thing.

## A note on deleting

You will not miss them. The reason the folder feels valuable is that it *might*
contain something good, and that feeling survives no matter how much you delete.
Keep the places. Keep the handful of recipes you have actually made. Let the rest
go.
