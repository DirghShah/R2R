You have been saving Lisbon videos for three months. You leave in two weeks. The
folder has sixty saves, no addresses, and at least four videos about the same
pastry shop. Here is how to turn that into something you can actually use on the
ground.

## Get everything onto one map first

Do not start by making an itinerary. Start by making a map, because until you can
see where things are relative to each other, any schedule you write will be
fiction.

Whatever tool you use — Google Maps lists by hand, or an app that reads the
videos — the goal for this stage is one map with every candidate on it. Do not
filter yet. Do not group yet. Just get the pins down.

You will immediately discover two things: that a third of your saves are in a
part of the city you were not planning to visit, and that you saved the same
place three times from three different creators.

## Group by neighbourhood, not by day

The instinct is to assign places to days. Resist it. Trips slip, weather happens,
and a schedule built on days breaks on the first afternoon.

Group by **neighbourhood** instead. Six places in Alfama, four in Príncipe Real,
three near the water. Then, each morning, pick a neighbourhood based on how you
feel and what is open. Everything nearby is already on the list.

This is the single biggest improvement most people can make to trip planning, and
it costs nothing.

## Check opening hours before you go, not on the day

Short-form video is unreliable about hours in a specific way: it never mentions
them. A creator visits at 2pm on a Tuesday and says nothing about the place being
closed Mondays, or shutting at 4, or only doing lunch on weekends.

Go through your map once, before you fly, and check hours on every place you
actually care about. Expect to lose about one in five — closed permanently,
closed that day, or moved. Better to find out now than at the door.

While you are there, check whether anywhere needs booking. The famous ones
usually do, and by the time you are in the city it is too late.

## Mark the two or three you would be sad to miss

Sixty saves is not sixty visits. A week of eating is maybe fourteen meals, and
some of those will be a sandwich because you are tired.

Pick the two or three places that were the actual reason you got excited about
the trip, and treat those as fixed. Book them. Everything else on the map is
optional, which is exactly what a map is good for — being there when you happen
to be nearby, not demanding to be visited.

## Keep the original videos attached

This matters more than it sounds. Standing outside a place at 8pm, you will not
remember why you saved it. Being able to re-watch the ten-second clip that made
you save it tells you what to order and whether it is worth the queue.

Any tool that discards the link is throwing away the most useful thing it had.

## Share the map with whoever you are travelling with

The worst version of trip planning is one person holding all the information in
an app the others cannot see. The second worst is a Google Doc nobody opens.

A shared map means everyone can add the place their friend recommended, and
nobody has to ask "what was that bakery called" at breakfast. If your tool
supports sharing by link, use it — the friction of getting other people to sign
up is what kills group planning.

## After the trip

Delete the ones you went to and did not like. Keep the ones you missed. Next
time you are in that city, the map is already built — and it is better than it
was, because it now contains what you learned rather than only what a stranger
told you.
